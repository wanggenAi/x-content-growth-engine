## What the current sample actually shows

Two candidate mechanisms are worth carrying forward, but only at `OBSERVATION` level:

1. **Concrete utility or access change may create a strong reader payoff.** Three fresh posts center a specific capability/access change: something a reader can now use, enter, create or access. This gives a fast answer to “what changed for me?”
2. **Specific high-stakes claims can attract attention without a self-mirroring hook.** Two fresh posts center AI behavior/control/safety claims. This is useful negative evidence against treating self-mirroring as a necessary condition for attention.

A third descriptive label is common across all five samples: they use **concrete named actors/products/actions**, not vague generic commentary.

These are not viral formulas. The sample is too small and too biased toward large official AI accounts.

## Important counterevidence

- The Google AI post has labeled engagement but substantially lower observed likes/reposts than the Anthropic behavior-report post. “Official AI account + product/news announcement” is therefore not sufficient by itself to explain stronger interaction.
- The ChatGPT mobile post has conflicting untimed third-party cumulative metrics. Canonical likes/reposts remain `null`; the pipeline preserves the conflicting observations instead of choosing the larger number.
- Satya Nadella's safety post is fresh and strongly corroborated as a real status permalink, but has no verified reach metric. It cannot be used as a high-distribution example merely because the author is prominent.

## Main confounders

- All five current samples come from globally prominent technology executives or official AI/product accounts.
- There is still **no matched ordinary/low-distribution control cohort**.
- Four of five records lack verified X view counts.
- The sample is concentrated in AI/technology, so topic effects and structural effects are entangled.
- Third-party aggregators and mirrors can be cached; source snapshot time is not the same thing as post time or metric time.

## Freshness hardening applied

The public-source registry now explicitly separates:

- X post `published_at`;
- corroborating page `source_snapshot_at`;
- engagement `metric_as_of`;
- crawler/cache freshness risk.

A generic public page that is visibly serving old content can no longer be treated as current metric evidence merely because the URL is live. Untimed labeled metrics may remain observations, but cannot be used as a time series. Impossible metric decreases across untimed caches force canonical values back to `null`.

## Next sampling priorities

1. Grow the fresh set to at least **10–20 additional real status IDs** before promoting any replicated pattern.
2. Deliberately collect **ordinary/low-distribution controls**, ideally same author or similar author scale, similar topic and similar post age.
3. Expand outside large AI-company accounts into Chinese tech creators, ordinary-person experience, work/money, social observation, reversal/oddity and history/culture.
4. Prefer free sources that expose a real X permalink plus explicitly labeled metrics and a trustworthy source snapshot time.
5. Seek verifiable view counts when available, but do not weaken evidence rules and do not use paid data services.
6. Once controls exist, test immediate payoff, specificity, information gap, self-relevance, psychological stake and opinion space while controlling author scale and age.

## Current conclusion

The primary blocker has shifted. It is **no longer “cannot find any fresh X posts.”** The current bottleneck is now **sample diversity + matched controls + better distribution evidence**. That is the next stage the collector and analyzer should optimize for.
